01

The foundation

Introduction & Purpose

MSOC UK proudly presents Carol Virunnu, an annual Christmas carol singing competition that brings together parishes and congregations from across the United Kingdom to celebrate the Birth of our Lord Jesus Christ through music, worship, creativity and fellowship.

Carol Virunnu began in 2014 as an online carol singing competition. It has since developed into a live-stage gathering that provides parish communities, singers, musicians, families, children and young people with a shared platform to express faith, develop talent and celebrate Christmas together.

1.1 Aims of Carol Virunnu

01

Faith & Worship

Celebrate the Birth of Christ and encourage participants to use their God-given talents in a way that glorifies God.

02

Fellowship & Unity

Strengthen relationships among parishes, clergy, families, youth and children across the wider MSOC UK community.

03

Music & Talent

Provide a meaningful platform for singers and musicians to develop and demonstrate vocal, musical and creative ability.

04

Next Generation

Encourage children and young people to grow in faith, confidence, teamwork and a sense of belonging within the Church.

05

Creativity & Expression

Encourage thoughtful musical arrangements, natural expression and clear communication of the meaning of Christmas.

06

Healthy Competition

Promote preparation, discipline, teamwork and excellence while maintaining fairness, mutual respect and Christian fellowship.

✦

1.2 Guiding Performance Principle

Teams are encouraged to connect the Birth of Christ in Bethlehem with the realities of human life today. This should be achieved through strong group singing, appropriate musical accompaniment, carefully chosen words and natural expression rather than through theatrical performance.

02

Before taking part

General Terms & Conditions

  • All participating teams must comply with the rules, regulations, timelines and instructions issued by the organisers.
  • Each participating team and every registered member must be approved by the Parish Vicar before participation.
  • Teams must cooperate with organisers, technical staff, stage coordinators and judges, and maintain a spirit of faith, fairness, discipline, mutual respect and fellowship throughout the event.
  • Participation confirms that the team has read, understood and accepted the official competition rules and judging framework.
  • The judges’ scoring decisions and final results are final and binding. Administrative or disciplinary matters remain subject to the organisers’ authority.

Teams must keep to the stated timings; they are not flexible. If the spoken presentation finishes in under 2 minutes, the team must move directly into the song to maintain the flow, while completing the song within its maximum 6-minute limit.

◉

6.2 Microphones

  • A maximum of 12 standard microphones with stands will be available.
  • Teams may choose to use fewer microphones according to their arrangement.
  • Once microphone positions and heights have been agreed during sound check, the microphones and stands must remain fixed during the entirety of the performance.
  • Microphones must not be removed from stands, carried or repositioned during the performance.
⌁

6.3 Instrument Connections

  • A maximum of 4 instrument connection cables will be available.
  • All instrumentalists must be registered members of the participating team.
  • Teams should design arrangements around the stated provision and should not assume additional connections or equipment will be available.
07

Principal musical component

Competition Song

The Competition Song, which must be sung in Malayalam, should demonstrate the team’s vocal quality, musical discipline, arrangement, teamwork and effective use of accompaniment.

7.1 Song Selection

  • Each team must perform one suitable Christmas Carol song of their own choice.
  • Original or less frequently performed Christmas Carol songs may bring freshness and variety; this is encouraged but is not a judging requirement.

7.2 Singing Expectations

The song must remain primarily a group singing performance. Judges will listen for the following musical qualities:

AreaWhat a strong performance should demonstrate
Accuracy & ControlSecure pitch, reliable timing, controlled breathing and consistency throughout the performance.
Tone & BlendPleasant voice quality, balanced vocal sections and a unified ensemble sound.
Harmony & DynamicsWell-tuned harmony, effective balance, appropriate dynamic contrast and confident modulation where used.
Clarity & ExpressionClear diction, meaningful phrasing, musical expression and communication of the text.
Detail & Ensemble DisciplineClean entries and endings, attentive listening, musical precision and well-rehearsed teamwork.

Short solo or lead sections are permitted where they contribute naturally to the arrangement. However, the overall performance must demonstrate the strength, balance and unity of the full team.

7.3 Vocal Arrangement

Teams may create their own vocal arrangements using combinations of male and female sections, smaller vocal groups, full-team singing, lead-and-response passages, short solos, harmony, layered entries, dynamic changes and suitable musical variations. Creative arrangement is encouraged where it enhances, rather than distracts from, the singing.

7.4 Live Music & Accompaniment

  • Live musical instruments are permitted within the technical limits stated in Section 6.
  • Accompaniment should support and enhance the vocals rather than overpower them.
  • The human voice must remain the central element of the performance.
  • The number of instruments used does not attract marks by itself. Judges assess the quality, musicality, suitability and balance of the accompaniment with the vocals.
×

7.5 Pre-Recorded Vocals

Pre-recorded lead vocals, backing vocals, choir voices, vocal harmonies or any other recorded vocal content must not be used. Backing tracks are only acceptable where they contain no recorded vocal material.

!

Backing-track notification

Team Captains must inform the organisers by 25th November 2026 if a backing track will be used.

08

Meaning and reflection

Christmas Message / Spoken Presentation

Every team must include a meaningful spoken Christmas message.

✦

Performance Theme

“Christ Is Born — Let Peace Begin With Us”

The performance should creatively connect the Birth of Christ with the message of peace, hope, love and unity in today’s world.

  • The spoken presentation must be performed before the Carol Song.
  • The maximum duration is 2 minutes.
  • Those delivering the spoken presentation must also sing in the Carol Song.

8.1 Content & Purpose

The spoken presentation should go beyond simply retelling the Nativity story. It should connect the message of Christmas with present-day human experience and may explore:

LovePeaceHopeFaithCompassionForgivenessFamilyLonelinessHuman struggleUnityCare for othersChrist’s light

8.2 Delivery

  • The spoken presentation may be delivered by two or more team members.
  • Permitted formats include narration, storytelling, reflective words, multiple voices or dialogue-style narration, provided it does not become theatrical acting.
  • Soft live instrumental background music and brief musical transitions may be used where they support the spoken content.
  • No pre-recorded spoken content is permitted.
  • The message should connect naturally with the Carol Song so that both elements form one integrated presentation.

10.1 Singing — 85 Marks

Singing

Singing is the primary component. Judges will assess pitch and timing, voice quality, blend, harmony, balance, dynamics, diction, expression, breathing control and overall musical performance. Marks reflect the quality and unity of the team rather than individual soloists.

Music & Accompaniment

Judges will assess the quality and suitability of the accompaniment, including the arrangement and how effectively it supports and blends with the vocals. The number or complexity of instruments will not automatically result in higher marks.

10.2 Presentation — 5 Marks

Judges will assess costumes, visual unity, confidence, natural expression, stage presence, energy and audience engagement. Presentation should enhance the carol performance without becoming theatrical or dramatic.

11

Fairness and decisions

Penalties, Compliance & Decisions

The organisers may apply an appropriate penalty where a team fails to comply with the published competition rules or technical requirements. Depending on the nature and seriousness of the breach, action may include a mark penalty, exclusion of an element from judging, exclusion from results or disqualification.

!

Examples of material breaches

Use of unregistered performers, prohibited pre-recorded vocals, prohibited theatrical elements, failure to observe technical restrictions, or other material breaches of the official rules.

Where a rule requires interpretation during the event, the organisers may issue a practical ruling to protect fairness, safety, timing and consistency. The judges’ scoring decisions and final competition results remain final and binding.

12

The heart of the event

Spirit of Carol Virunnu

Carol Virunnu is more than a competition. It is a celebration of faith, fellowship, unity, disciplined preparation and the joyful proclamation of Christmas.

Teams are encouraged to pursue excellence without losing sight of the purpose for which the event exists.

Above all, Christ and the message of His Birth must remain at the heart of every performance.
